# Strive Elite: aim (who the offer is for, before step 4 naming)

Written Oct 14 2026 on Coach Yinka's "we need to aim the offer better".
The method is the chapter before the offer build in $100M Offers: pick
the starving crowd (pain, purchasing power, easy to reach, growing),
then niche down so the same product reads as made for one family, not
for all of youth soccer. Nothing here is live until he stamps it.

## Where the aim is today

Every piece of live copy aims at the whole youth soccer market:

- /demo headline: "Team training 3-4x a week is not close to enough to
  achieve your goals." Half an aim (it presumes a player who already
  trains 3 to 4 times a week) but it never names the player or the goal.
- Ads 1 to 5: "your player", "a 13-year-old", "every kid". Cold audience
  on the sheet is parents 30 to 55 with youth soccer interests.
- Fair questions: generic ("Will they actually do it?", "We already
  train 3-4 times a week", "What if we miss a week?").
- Nowhere does the page say who it is NOT for.

This is the crowd that has already said yes at $249. The research file
(Sept 14) had it as a hypothesis: "stalled or uncertain, roughly 11-14"
and "self-directed club goal, 15-18". The real buyers sit across the
seam of those two, and they are not stalled. They are ambitious.

## The starving-crowd test on that crowd

- Pain: real and specific. The player trains 3 to 4 times a week with
  the club and the weak foot or first touch is not moving, because team
  practice cannot work on one kid's gap. Privates are $80 and the slots
  are gone. The parent cannot coach it. (Problem list, Oct 11.)
- Purchasing power: they already pay club fees (research benchmarks:
  FVA Union about $4,245 a year, travel about $1,550 a year). $249 a
  month sits inside what they already spend on soccer, not on top of a
  rec fee.
- Easy to reach: they are the Instagram audience we already have (about
  230k views a month, all clients from IG). On Meta they are one
  interest set away: MLS NEXT, ECNL, club soccer, plus the named NoVA
  clubs. The GHL survey already captures player age.
- Growing: club soccer in NoVA is consolidating upward (MLS NEXT, ECNL
  expansion in the research), and every one of those rosters is a
  tryout a year away for every kid on it.

## The aim, proposed

One sentence, for the page, the ads, the DM qualifying text and the
call: Strive Elite is for the 12 to 15 year old already on a competitive
club team who wants to make the next level, and for the parent who can
see the weak foot and the first touch not moving between practices.

Dream outcome in that family's words (public-safe versions, "dominant"
stays call language per the Oct 11 flag): "the player the next-level
coach can't leave out", "ready for the tryout, not hoping for it",
"noticeably better in 6 weeks, a different player in 12".

Who it is NOT for, said on the page, because that is the filter:
- a player under 12 or still deciding whether he likes soccer (the
  research warns against early urgency; we agree);
- a player who will not train alone, with no parent willing to read one
  text a week;
- a 16 to 18 year old on a college clock who needs film and calls (that
  is the assessment-call conversation, not the page).

Why this aim and not the two alternatives:
- The "benched or stalled 11 to 14" segment has the strongest emotion in
  the research, but none of our five buyers is benched, and leading with
  it reads as "your club failed you", which the research says not to do
  (coach satisfaction is high nationally).
- The "college-track 15 to 18" segment was the Complete Pathway, retired
  Oct 12. Elite has no film room, so the page would be promising the
  wrong product to them.

## What the eleven-item stack looks like through this aim

Every stack item lands harder once the avatar is this specific:

- In-season load as the default (item 1): a club player is in season
  nine months a year; 4 x 40 is "train less, aim better", not "add
  hours".
- A ball and a few yards (item 3): the townhouse family on a club team
  is exactly who asks "do we have the space".
- Strengthening finishers (item 9): 12 to 15 is the growth-spurt window
  where the knees and hips start to complain; the parent already fears
  overuse on top of club.
- The two-week proof clip (decided Oct 14): the parent of a club player
  watches film anyway; two clips of the same drill is proof in a
  language they already speak.
- Accountability Zooms and the community (items 2 and 10): a room of
  other club players chasing the same next level is a reason to show
  up that a rec player's parent would not pay for.
- Film IQ (item 11, still conflicting with the Elite boundary): the
  next-level player is the one whose game reading gets judged at a
  tryout. If it ever comes into Elite, this is the avatar it is for.

## What changes the moment it is stamped (copy debt, phase C)

- /demo: headline names the player ("Your club player trains 3 to 4
  times a week. His weak foot still isn't moving."), sub-headline
  carries the aim sentence, one "Is this for your player?" block with
  the three not-for lines, fair questions gain "He's 12, is this too
  much on top of club?" and "He has a tryout in the spring."
- Ads: the one primary text (Oct 10) opens on the club player, not "your
  player trains hard at practice"; the Meta audience narrows to MLS
  NEXT, ECNL, club soccer and the named NoVA clubs with a player-age
  filter where Meta allows it. Ad 4's "a 13-year-old" already fits.
- Carla's first text (sales process, Sept 16): the three questions add
  club and level before stage, goal and gap, so the call opens knowing
  whether the family is the avatar.
- GHL intake survey: confirm it asks club and current level, not only
  age (lives in GHL, unreachable from here).
- The VSL cannot be re-cut; its "who it's for" opener was written for
  the Complete avatar (14 to 18, college or academy goal) and is close
  enough to carry until a second VSL.
- Nothing in the app changes. Onboarding already collects age, club,
  level, position and goals, which is how this table exists.

## Decisions for Coach Yinka

1. Stamp the aim sentence as written, or edit the age band (12 to 15 is
   the buyers; 11 to 16 is the widest the data supports).
2. Boys and girls, or boys. Every buyer is a boy; the IG audience split
   is unconfirmed. Recommendation: keep "your player" and he/she by the
   gender field, aim the pictures and examples at boys.
3. Say who it is NOT for on the page, or keep that to the call.
4. The public dream-outcome line: "the player the next-level coach
   can't leave out", "ready for the tryout", or another of his own.

Say the numbers and the aim goes into the phase C page rewrite, the
"we" ad pass, Carla's first text and the ad sheet together, with the
$500 copy, not before.
