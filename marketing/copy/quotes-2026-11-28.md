# 70 checked quotes for the training app

## The example quote: "The man who loves walking..."

What I found: no traceable original. The wording that circulates is "The man who loves walking will walk further than the man who loves the destination." Quote sites (Goodreads, Quotefancy) credit Sal Di Stefano, the Mind Pump fitness podcast host, but none cites an episode or a page of his book. The "Don't Fear Grit" podcast says the line "has been around for a few years" and was given new life when Di Stefano used it on a podcast (around 2022), so he popularized it rather than wrote it. Blogs also credit Lao Tzu, with no text cited; it is not a known line of the Tao Te Ching. One blog lists it as "Anonymous". Verdict: an unattributed modern saying. It is NOT in the 70. If the owner wants to use it, the honest credit is "Unknown" (or "popularized by Sal Di Stefano").

## How sure I am

Overall: confident on the wording and speaker for about 55 of the 70; each was found in the outlet named, usually a dated speech, interview or book. But this session could not open web pages directly (Wikiquote, Quote Investigator and most sites were blocked), so every check went through a web search tool that reads the pages and reports what they say; nothing was read with my own eyes on the page, and a spot check by a person before anything ships is worth doing.
Least sure, check these first: #8 (Lloyd, one journal Q&A), #9 (Morgan, Nike page, no dated origin), #16 (Gerrard, wording varies), #21 (Ali Adnan, the exact FIFA page is my best match), #24 (Serena, via TIME's citation of The National), #34 (Hamilton, no dated origin), #39 (Fraser-Pryce "Resilience...", a headline), #48 (Jordan ad, transcripts differ slightly and a copywriter wrote it), #58 (Wooden "Failure is not fatal", reached only through a copy of Wikiquote), #59 (Walsh, a book title), #63 (Bannister, no dated origin).
