# The copy quiz: finding the app's voice

Coach Yinka, Nov 27 2026: "id like to diffrientiate the app by speaking in
copy our user resonates with. and im the perfect person to quiz for that
since i know the consumer in and out because i was just the consumer last
year." Nothing too different from what the app says today.

## How it runs

- Ten real moments in the app, one at a time.
- Each moment is written four ways, each a small step in a different
  direction: plain, a teammate talking, cold and short, the fact with a
  number.
- Numbering: moment N, versions NA to ND, and three questions every time:
  - Q1. Which one makes you open the app?
  - Q2. Which one makes you turn notifications off (or cringe)?
  - Q3. How would you actually have texted it to a teammate?
- At the end: a one-page voice guide (the rules, the words we use, the
  words we never use, ten model lines), added to the COPY RULE with a test,
  then the app is rewritten to it.

## Moment 1: a friend just passed you on the board (a push)

- 1A. "Nico just passed you. One session takes it back."
- 1B. "Nico just went past you. You good with that?"
- 1C. "Nico passed you. Your move."
- 1D. "Nico's ahead of you now. He trained 45 minutes today."

His answers:
- Q1 (opens the app): 1B and 1D.
- Q2 (turns notifications off): 1C.
- Q3 (his own words): "bro fell off his training, bros not locked, theres a
  new king to the gc"

What it says about the voice (my reading, to be checked against the next
moments):
- A teammate's jab, asked as a question, works. So does a plain fact with
  a number in it.
- A cold slogan ("Your move.") is the turn-off: it sounds like a brand, not
  a friend.
- His own words are group chat talk: "locked", "fell off", "king of the gc".
  Status among friends is the prize.

Used so far: the push itself now reads "Nico just passed you" / "Trained
45m just now. You good with that?" (the fact, then the jab).

## Moment 2: you just finished training and posted it

- 2A. "Posted. 45 minutes on your week."
- 2B. "45 minutes. That's how you do it."
- 2C. "45 minutes in. You just passed Nico."
- 2D. "Locked in. 45 minutes on the board."

His answers:
- Q1 (would screenshot): 2C, "but none are really good". Better, his own:
  "Posted, continue outworking the competition" or "45 minutes locked in,
  excellent work".
- Q2 (cringe): none. But 2D and 2B are "too plain if you're trying to
  motivate or instigate". 2A is plain too, but "professional or standard".
- Q3 (his own words): "lets go boys another sesh in the books", with "but
  im also a guy lol".

What it says about the voice:
- Every line has a job: MOTIVATE or INSTIGATE. A line that only reports is
  plain. Plain is acceptable where the app is being professional (a
  receipt, a setting), never where it is meant to move the player.
- Praise is welcome and said straight: "excellent work".
- "The competition" is the frame: you are outworking someone.
- "Locked in" lands when it carries the number and the praise with it, not
  on its own.
- His lines are a little longer and warmer than my clipped ones.
- He flagged it himself: he is a guy, and the app is for girls too. So no
  "boys", "bro" or "king" in the app's own voice; the energy without the
  gendered words.

## Moment 3: mid-week, the other club is ahead in the matchup

- 3A. "Haymarket 08s is ahead by 12 minutes per player." (the app today)
- 3B. "Haymarket 08s is up 12 minutes a player. Is your club letting that
  happen?"
- 3C. "12 minutes a player. That's all Haymarket 08s has on your club. One
  session each closes it."
- 3D. "Haymarket 08s is outworking your club this week. 12 minutes a
  player."

His answers:
- Q1 (makes him train or text the club): 3B. Also his own: "You're getting
  outworked by Haymarket, let's go." And "(club name) works nicely": saying
  the club's own name in the line. "There should be a mix."
- Q2 (cringe): none.
- Q3 (what he would send the club chat): 3B.

What it says about the voice:
- The question jab wins a second time ("Is your club letting that happen?").
- "Getting outworked" plus "let's go" is the other shape he reaches for:
  the hard fact, then the rally.
- Name the club (and the other club) in the line. Names make it theirs.
- A MIX: the same moment should not always say the same sentence. A few
  lines per moment, rotated.

## Moment 4: the one upgrade card on the Week tab (Strive Pro)

- 4A. "A week built around your weak foot. Four sessions a week, one drill
  at a time with the video." (the app today)
- 4B. "You're putting in the hours. Are they fixing your weak foot?"
- 4C. "You train hard. Train on the right thing. A week built around your
  weak foot."
- 4D. "4 hours this week, excellent work. Now aim it. A week built around
  your weak foot."

His answers:
- Q1 (makes him tap): "4D is good". Better, "something more logical like:
  congrats on the hard work, but make sure you aren't rowing your boat in
  the wrong direction."
- Q2 (feels like an ad): "all except 4a", and when asked which way he
  meant it: "4a doesnt feel like an ad". So 4B, 4C and 4D all read as ads
  to him, 4D included, even though its shape (praise, then aim it) is the
  one he liked. The ask has to sound like the app talking, not an advert.
- Q3 (how he would tell a teammate what Strive Pro is), his words, which
  he called way too long: "you are able to input your exact situation into
  an ai coach, that will assign you sessions from the strive drill bank
  that will actually make you improve on what you need, not whats
  comfortable. its very easy to follow and has videos to every session.
  you also have access to full courses for specific areas you are
  struggling in. like weak foot, speed, ball mastery, or even strength
  training, this way you always have the best drills to work on when your
  at the field."

What it says about the voice:
- For the ask, LOGIC beats hype: praise the work, then the reason (effort
  aimed the wrong way is wasted). A picture helps ("rowing your boat in the
  wrong direction").
- Lines to keep, his: "what you need, not what's comfortable"; "very easy
  to follow"; "videos to every session"; "the best drills to work on when
  you're at the field".
- FLAG, his to rule on: he says "AI coach" when he explains it to a
  teammate. The standing rule (Policies, personalization) is never "AI",
  "automated" or "algorithm" in anything a player or parent reads. Asked
  him whether that rule holds for Strive Pro's own copy.

## Moment 5: a friend just posted their training (a push)

- 5A. "Diego just posted" / "Diego's post opens when you post yours
  today." (the app today)
- 5B. "Diego just trained 1h 15m" / "Post yours to see it."
- 5C. "Diego's getting work in. 1h 15m today." / "You training today?"
- 5D. "Diego is outworking you today" / "1h 15m. Let's go."

His answers:
- Q1 (opens the app): 5C and 5D.
- Q2 (turns notifications off): 5A, the line the app sends today.
- Q3 (what he would text a teammate who just trained): "lets go dawg keep
  it up", "lets work", "on the come up".
- Then, on his own words: "but it also has to appeal to girls".

What it says about the voice:
- The number is the hook ("1h 15m today"). A push that hides it behind a
  rule ("opens when you post yours") is the turn-off.
- Both shapes work again: the question ("You training today?") and the
  hard fact with the rally ("outworking you today", "Let's go").
- To a friend who trained he is SUPPORTIVE first: "keep it up", "let's
  work", "on the come up". The jab is for the one who is not training.
- IT HAS TO APPEAL TO GIRLS, his rule, said twice now. The energy stays;
  "dawg", "bro", "boys", "king" stay out of the app's own voice. Worth
  doing before the rewrite ships: read the ten model lines to two girls he
  has coached.
- NOT CHANGED YET: 5A is live today. It is rewritten with the rest at the
  end of the quiz.

### Who the voice is for (his words, Nov 28 2026)

"more for boys but also can work with girls". So the voice leans toward
how boys talk in a group chat, and every line still has to read fine to a
girl: competitive energy, jabs and rallies stay; a word that only fits a
boy ("boys", "bro", "king", "dawg") stays out of what the app itself says.

## Moment 6: the text the app writes for a player to send a teammate

- 6A. "Train with me on Strive Elite. My code: KAI123" (the app today)
- 6B. "I'm putting my training on Strive now. Add me so we can see who's
  really working. Code: KAI123"
- 6C. "Bet you can't outwork me this week. Get on Strive. Code: KAI123"
- 6D. "We're all tracking our training on Strive. You in? Code: KAI123"

His answers:
- Q1 (would send): 6C. Or his own: "let's grind on Strive Elite."
- Q2, Q3: not answered.

What it says about the voice:
- The invite is a CHALLENGE ("Bet you can't outwork me this week") or a
  rally ("Let's grind"), never an announcement.
- "Outwork" is his word in four of six moments now. "Grind" joins "locked
  in", "getting work in", "on the come up", "let's go", "let's work".

### Open: the word "AI"

Asked twice whether "never say AI" still holds for Strive Pro's own words
(he said "AI coach" when describing it to a teammate). Not answered, so
the standing rule holds: no "AI", "automated" or "algorithm" anywhere a
player or parent reads.
