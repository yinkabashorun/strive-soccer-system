# Strive Elite follow-up playbook

Drafted Nov 17 2026 for Coach Yinka and Carla. DRAFT: nothing here has been
sent. Every message is written to be pasted into GHL or an Instagram DM.

## The one structure, for everyone

Three gives, then one ask. Repeat.

Every message is one of four kinds:

1. HELPFUL. One small, specific thing they can use today. Nothing to buy.
2. HUMAN. Funny or warm. Reads like a person, not a business.
3. PROBLEM, THEN US. Their problem in their words, then Strive Elite as the answer.
4. GIFT. Something free that is worth having (the dribbling course, the weak foot test).

Rules that make them work:

- One idea per message. If it needs a second paragraph, it is two messages.
- End on a question they can answer in one word. A reply is the goal, not a sale.
- The moment they reply, the sequence stops and a person answers within the hour.
- Never "just checking in" or "following up". Every message has its own reason to exist.
- No price in a text. The call does the selling.
- No guilt. A no-show or a quiet parent is never told they did something wrong.
- "Your player", never "your child" or "your kid". "We" when it is Coach Gary and Coach Yinka.
- No sign-off, no emojis, first names only.

## 1. Zoom no-shows (the warmest people we have)

They wanted the call. Something got in the way. Make coming back easy.

10 minutes after the missed call. HUMAN.
> Hey [Parent], we had [Player]'s call down for [time] today. No worries at all, things come up. Want me to send two new times?

Next day. HELPFUL.
> Hey [Parent], something to try with [Player] while we find a new time. 30 seconds of touches with the strong foot, then 30 with the weak foot. Count both. The gap between the two numbers tells us a lot. What did [Player] get?

Day 3. GIFT.
> Hey [Parent], I'd like [Player] to have our dribbling course, free. It's the one our members use. Want me to send the link?

Day 7. HUMAN, funny.
> Hey [Parent], I'm guessing one of three things happened. 1. Life got busy. 2. [Player] signed a pro contract and no longer needs us. 3. My texts are going to spam. If it's number 1, send me a day that works and I'll make it easy.

After that they move to the monthly helpful list (section 5).

## 2. Old in-person clients (parents)

They already know and like you. Lead with the relationship, not the product.
In-person sessions have ended, so the honest reason to write is: here is
what we built for players like yours.

Day 0. HUMAN.
> Hey [Parent], Coach Yinka here. I was thinking about [Player] this week. How is the season going?

Day 3. HELPFUL.
> Hey [Parent], this one gets asked a lot, so here it is. 5 snacks for the car on the way to a game: a banana, pretzels, half a peanut butter and jelly sandwich, an applesauce pouch, dry cereal. Plus water. Nothing greasy and nothing new on game day. What does [Player] usually eat before a game?

Day 7. PROBLEM, THEN US.
> Hey [Parent], the question I got most from our in-person families was "what should [Player] be doing between sessions?" Coach Gary and I built the answer. It's called Strive Elite: [Player]'s own training for the week, built around what [Player] needs, done at home. Want to see what [Player]'s would look like?

Day 14. GIFT.
> Hey [Parent], we're giving our dribbling course to families who trained with us, free. Want me to send it for [Player]?

## 3. Parents who showed interest and went quiet

They do not know you yet. Earn it. One helpful message a week, an ask every
fourth week.

Week 1. HELPFUL.
> Hey [Parent], a quick one for game days. The car ride home decides whether your player wants to talk to you about soccer. One question works better than any feedback: "What was your favorite part of the game?" Try it this weekend and tell me what you hear.

Week 2. HELPFUL.
> Hey [Parent], if [Player] has a tryout or a big game coming up: nothing new the night before. Same dinner, same bedtime, bag packed before bed. The players who look calm on the day are the ones whose night before was boring. Anything big coming up for [Player]?

Week 3. HUMAN, funny.
> Hey [Parent], honest question. Does [Player] have a weak foot, or a foot that is just there for balance? Most players I meet have the second one.

Week 4. PROBLEM, THEN US.
> Hey [Parent], team practice is built for the whole team. It can't spend 20 minutes on the one thing holding your player back. That is the part Coach Gary and I do. Want to get on a call and find out what that one thing is for [Player]?

## 4. Players (Instagram DM, 13 and older)

Short. A challenge, not a pitch. Players under 13 are reached through the parent.

> Weak foot only juggling. What's your record? Send the number. No lying.

> Honest question. When did you last use your weak foot in a game on purpose?

> What is the one part of your game you'd fix if you could fix it by Saturday?

When they answer the last one:
> That's fixable. Coach Gary and I build players a plan around exactly that. Want me to send the video that explains it? Have a parent watch it with you.

## 5. The helpful list (one a month, to everyone who has gone quiet)

Each of these is one message. Niche on purpose: it shows we think about the
whole player, not just the drills.

1. 5 snacks for the car on the way to a game or practice.
2. What to do the night before a tryout.
3. What to say on the car ride home, and what not to say.
4. What goes in the bag the night before (and the one thing players always forget).
5. A 10 minute warm-up for a cold morning game.
6. How to film a game on a phone so a coach can actually use it.
7. The 60 second weak foot test (already drafted: marketing/lead-magnets/weak-foot-test.md).
8. A 20 minute session for a night with a lot of homework.
9. Three questions to ask the club coach at the end of the season.
10. What to drink at halftime.

Food and drink messages stay general: no calorie numbers, no supplements, no
weight talk, and a family with an allergy swaps the item. It is not medical
advice and never claims to be.

## 6. The dribbling course as the free gift

The gift message never sends the link first. It asks "want me to send it?"
so the reply opens a conversation. The link goes out with one line:

> Here it is: [link]. Start with the first video. When [Player] has done a week of it, tell me how it went and I'll tell you what to work on next.

The course ends on the video and the call. A week later:

> Hey [Parent], how is [Player] getting on with the dribbling course? If you want, we can get on a call and I'll show you what the next three months would look like for [Player].

## 7. Twenty more, ready to send (Nov 17 2026)

Written on Coach Yinka's "yes i love these. i need you to write 20 follow ups".
Each one stands alone. Mix them three gives to one ask.

### Helpful (8)

1. The game bag. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], small thing that saves a lot of Saturday mornings. Have [Player] pack the bag the night before, and put a second pair of socks in it. Wet socks at halftime make for a long second half. Does [Player] pack their own bag yet?

2. Game morning. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], a game day one. A normal breakfast at the table a couple of hours before kickoff beats anything eaten in the car on the way. Toast, eggs, fruit, whatever [Player] already eats. Nothing new on game day. Is [Player] a breakfast eater, or is it a fight every morning?

3. The homework night. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], for the nights when homework takes over. 20 minutes is enough: 10 minutes of touches on the ball, then 10 minutes on the weak foot only. A short session that gets done beats a long one that gets skipped. Which nights are the busiest for [Player]?

4. Filming a game. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], if you film [Player]'s games, three things make the video useful to a coach. Stand as high as you can near the halfway line. Hold the phone sideways. Keep the players around the ball in the frame instead of zooming in on [Player]. Do you film the games?

5. Cold mornings. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], cold morning games are coming. Have [Player] jog and skip for five minutes before touching a ball, then get touches, then two short sprints. Players who start warm play the first ten minutes. Players who start cold watch them. What time are [Player]'s games this month?

6. The one question for the club coach. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], the best question to ask [Player]'s club coach this season is a short one: "What is the one thing [Player] needs to improve to help the team more?" Coaches respect it, and you get a straight answer. Have you ever asked it?

7. Watching a game on TV. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], next time a game is on TV, try this with [Player]. Pick one player in [Player]'s position and watch only them for ten minutes. Not the ball. Where they stand, where they look, when they move. What position does [Player] play?

8. After a rough game. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], for the drive home after a rough game. The feedback can wait until tomorrow. The one line that works tonight is "I love watching you play." How does [Player] usually take a bad game?

### Funny (4)

9. The sideline. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], quick poll. How many times did you say "use your other foot" from the sideline last weekend? No judgment. Every sideline has one, and most of us have been it.

10. Backyard and Saturday. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], does [Player] also turn into a different player between the backyard and Saturday's game? Brilliant at home, quiet on the field. Most parents I talk to say yes. Is that [Player] too?

11. The empty goal. Players, by DM.
> Be honest. How many of your training sessions this month were just shooting on an empty goal?

12. The last one. Anyone who never replied.
> Hey [Parent], last text from me, I promise. I'd rather be the coach who stopped texting than the coach who couldn't take a hint. If [Player] ever wants a plan built around their game, you know where to find us.

### Their problem, then Strive Elite (5)

13. Working hard, not changing. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], if [Player] trains hard and still looks like the same player as six months ago, it is usually not effort. It is training that is a bit of everything. Coach Gary and I find the one thing holding [Player] back and build the week around it. Want to find out what that one thing is?

14. Only goes one way. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], if defenders have figured out that [Player] only goes one way, [Player] already knows it. It shows up as fewer touches and safer passes. A weak foot does not fix itself at team practice. It needs its own work every week, and that is what Coach Gary and I build. Want to see what that would look like for [Player]?

15. No time. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], between school, practice and games, nobody has room for another two hour session. Strive Elite is built for that: short sessions at home that fit around homework, planned for [Player] so no time goes on guessing what to do. Would that fit [Player]'s week?

16. Keeping score. Any parent.
> Hey [Parent], you shouldn't have to be the one keeping track of whether [Player] trained. With Strive Elite you get a text when the new week is ready, and Coach Gary and I see every session [Player] finishes. Is the reminding at your house mostly on you right now?

17. Coming back from an injury. ONLY a family whose player is injured or returning.
> Hey [Parent], once the doctor or PT clears [Player] to train with the ball, there is a window before full contact. Players either lose ground in it or come back sharper than they left. Coach Gary and I build the training for exactly that window, around what [Player] is cleared to do. Where is [Player] in the recovery?

### A gift (3)

18. The dribbling course.
> Hey [Parent], I'd like [Player] to have our dribbling course, free. It is the one our members use to get dangerous on the ball. Want me to send the link?

19. The 60 second weak foot test.
> Hey [Parent], a free one that takes 60 seconds. [Player] does 30 seconds of touches with the strong foot, then 30 with the weak foot, and counts both. Send me the two numbers and I'll tell you what they mean. Want to try it tonight?

20. One clip, one fix. No-shows and old clients first: it costs coach time.
> Hey [Parent], send me a 20 second clip of [Player] with the ball, any drill or any game. I'll watch it and tell you the one thing I'd work on first. No cost. Want to send one?

Notes on these twenty: 17 goes only to a family who has told us about an
injury, and never says we treat or heal anything. 20 is real coach time per
reply, so start it with no-shows and old clients. 2 stays general food
guidance, not medical advice. None of them names a price, a guarantee or a
number of spots.
