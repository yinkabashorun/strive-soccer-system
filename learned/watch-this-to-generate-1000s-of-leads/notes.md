## Notes by timestamp

- 0:00 Most sites send traffic straight to "buy" or "get a quote". Most
  visitors are not ready, leave, and never come back.
- 1:07 A direct "book a call" works only for an informed audience that
  already got value from you. For less warm traffic, give a better reason
  to hand over contact details. The real job of "book a call" is lead
  capture.
- 1:38 Offer a mini offer: a complete solution to a narrow problem, free or
  low cost, that reveals the next problem your core offer solves. People who
  take the free thing are more likely to buy the related paid thing later.
- 2:41 Same 100 people: "buy my thing" gets about 1; "let me help you first,
  then want to buy?" can get 3 to 5 times that. No extra traffic, higher
  conversion.
- 3:50 His story: a webinar failed; a "free case study" screen recording
  with a specific result headline filled the calendar the next morning.
  Sophisticated audiences read a webinar as a pitch; "watch it on your own
  time" lowers the guard.
- 4:58 A failed lead magnet means that magnet failed, not the tactic. Test
  the packaging (headline) harder than the contents; the name can move
  response 2 to 10x, provided the contents clearly solve a problem.
- 5:28 Why it works: the Costco sample. Three types: sample or trial, one
  step of a multi-step process, an assessment that reveals a problem.
- 7:12 Pay with time now, more likely to pay with money later.
- 8:13 Freebie seekers: only give it to qualified people; add a qualifying
  question and route the rest elsewhere.
- 8:43 Don't solve the core problem for free; solve one that leads to it, so
  they are left deprived of the thing you sell. Entree then dessert; sell
  water to the thirsty, food after.
- 10:24 Type 1, reveal a problem: show the gap and the delta to solved;
  best for problems that get worse while waiting (posture, termites,
  taxes). His best ad: "4 reasons you'll never have a million-dollar
  agency". Be very specific on the negatives, vaguer on the positives.
- 12:58 Type 2, free trial: give it, then the removal creates the want.
  Limit uses, quantity or time. Example: four free months of agency
  service, then paid.
- 15:17 Type 3, one step of many: first coat of paint, first laser session
  of six to eight, first videos of a course.
- 15:50 Four delivery methods: software or tools (calculators, assessment
  tools, URL checker), information (mini courses, guides, expert
  interviews, templates; scalable, no operational drag), services (free
  done-for-you work for qualified leads only; math: $25 hard cost, one in
  four buys, $100 to acquire a customer), physical products (a "CEO" shirt
  only for proven CEOs gives a qualified list). Combine freely.
- 18:30 Ideal lead: budget, authority, clear need, wants to act now. He
  trained people free for a year for testimonials; almost all then paid.
- 22:22 Naming matters most for engagement. Advertise the result, not the
  vehicle ("Big Booty Boot Camp", not "six-week deadlift and squat
  seminar"). Different names attract different people.
- 24:02 Test names: small ad split tests; or a story poll ("if you're not X,
  don't answer"), comment A or B, or text people. He split-tested six book
  titles, images and subheads.
- 25:42 Name formats: number + outcome + time frame; how to [good] without
  [bad] even if [insecurity]; the [adjective] [thing] that [good]; X
  mistakes that keep you from [good].
- 26:47 Most people forget the call to action, or ask once. The salespeople
  who ask most close most. Earn more asks by giving value between them.
- 27:52 CTA formula: clear not clever; the exact next action; a reason to do
  it now (scarcity or urgency if you have it). Any reason beats none (the
  line-cutting study). Service businesses have real capacity limits: say
  so, or run rolling cohorts.
- 31:00 Recap: give value first (reveal, trial, one step), deliver it
  (software, information, service, physical), name it well or ask the
  audience, and put CTAs before, inside and after the magnet.
