# Strive Elite: Problem list (Step 2 of the offer build)

The method: list every thing a family must DO to get the result, in order,
and for each one write the four problems in the prospect's own words,
one per value driver: (1) it's hard, confusing, I won't like it (effort),
(2) it takes too much time (time), (3) it's expensive, not worth it
(dream outcome), (4) I won't be able to keep it up, life gets in the way
(likelihood). Written from the parent's side. Player voice where the
player is the one who has to do it. Step 3 (solutions) turns every line
below into a feature, a rule, or a piece of copy.

Dream outcome, stated plainly: my player gets noticeably better between
practices, without me having to coach it, and I can see it happening.

## First thing they must do: Decide this is worth trying

1. I don't really understand what this is. An app? Videos? Is this just
   YouTube drills with a price tag? I don't know what we'd actually get.
2. I don't have time to research another soccer thing. The season is
   already a second job.
3. $249 a month on top of club fees, and he already trains three or four
   times a week. If he's not getting better now, why would more work fix
   it?
4. We've bought programs before and used them for two weeks. This will be
   the same. And it's remote, so who is actually going to make him do it?

## Next thing they must do: Sign up and get set up at home

1. What does he need? A wall? Cones? A yard? We live in a townhouse. I
   don't know if our space works and I don't want to buy equipment to
   find out.
2. Setting up an account, onboarding, downloading something, filling in
   forms. One more login I have to manage.
3. If the first week doesn't fit us, I've already paid for the month.
4. He's 13. He won't set anything up himself. If it depends on me to get
   it going, it won't get going.

## Next thing they must do: Make time for four sessions a week

1. Four sessions on top of team practice and games. Where in the week do
   those even go? Which days? How long?
2. 40 minutes a session is a lot for a kid with homework. We'll miss one
   and then it falls apart.
3. If he only does two of the four, am I paying full price for half a
   program?
4. Tournament weekends, travel, a cold week, a sick week. Real life will
   eat the schedule and the plan won't know.

## Next thing they must do: Do the session right, alone

1. He'll watch the video and still do it wrong. Nobody is there to
   correct him. I can't correct him, I don't know what right looks like.
2. He'll rush through it in 15 minutes and say he's done.
3. If he does it wrong for a month, we paid to build bad habits.
4. He'll get bored. The same drills every week and he'll stop caring.
   Then it's a fight at home, and the fight isn't worth $249.

## Next thing they must do: Keep the kid accountable (parent)

1. I don't want to be the enforcer. I'm already the ride, the laundry,
   the fee payer. Adding "did you do your soccer homework" ruins the one
   thing he loves.
2. I don't have time to check an app every day.
3. If I have to police it, I'm the one doing the work, not the program.
4. I'll start strong and stop checking by week three. Then nobody knows
   if he's doing it.

## Next thing they must do: Know it's working

1. How do I know he's improving? I'm not a coach. "Better first touch"
   means nothing to me.
2. It will take months to see anything in a game and by then I've paid
   for months.
3. If I can't see progress, I can't justify the money. I need proof, not
   a feeling.
4. His club coach doesn't know about any of this. If nothing changes in
   his playing time, I'll assume it didn't work.

## Next thing they must do: Keep going month after month

1. Month two is harder than month one. The novelty is gone and nothing
   is forcing us.
2. Cancelling is probably a hassle. Programs make it hard on purpose.
3. If the price goes up or the rate changes, I'll feel tricked.
4. The season ends, the holidays hit, a new team, a new coach. Every
   transition is an exit.

## Next thing they must do: Turn it into games (player)

1. I can do the move in the backyard. In a game there's a defender and I
   freeze.
2. My coach doesn't run my team the way these drills work. It won't fit.
3. I put in all this work and still don't start. What was the point.
4. Nobody on my team is doing this. It feels like extra. It feels like I
   am behind, not ahead.

## What this list says about the offer (for step 3)

- The two biggest fears are not price. They are "who makes him do it"
  (likelihood) and "I can't tell if it's working" (dream outcome). Both
  are things Strive Elite already does: a text to the parent every week,
  completion visible in the app, a week that picks back up after a miss.
  The copy has to say so in the parent's words, not in feature words.
- "Hard, confusing, I'll do it wrong" is answered by a video for every
  drill plus three drills per session, not twenty. Say the number.
- "Life gets in the way" is answered by month to month, same-day refund,
  and the plan resuming without a makeup schedule. Already on the page.
- Space and equipment are a real unanswered worry. Nothing on the page
  says "a ball, a few cones, a patch of grass or a driveway; a wall is a
  bonus, not a requirement". Add it.
- The game-transfer fear is the player's fear and nobody is talking to
  the player. One line aimed at him is worth having.
- Every line above is a candidate objection for the sales call, in order.
