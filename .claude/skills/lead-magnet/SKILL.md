# Lead Magnet

## When To Use This

- Parents follow Strive or visited thestriveapp.com/demo but did not book a call. A bare "book a call" only works on people who already know Strive and got value from it.
- Coach Yinka wants more booked assessment calls from the same followers and traffic, with no extra ad spend.
- Naming, testing or writing the call to action for any free offer.

## The Method

1. Write the job in one line: this free thing exists to capture a parent's contact details (and, where possible, a booked call) from someone not ready to buy yet. Write who it is for in one line (for example: parent of a 12 to 15 year old on a competitive club team). Build for that parent only.
2. Pick a narrow problem the free thing fully solves, where solving it reveals the next problem Strive Elite solves. Never give away the core thing that is sold (the week built around the player). Check: after using it, is the parent hungrier for the paid thing, not satisfied?
3. Choose the type, or combine them:
   a. Reveal a problem (the strongest): a test or assessment that shows a gap the parent did not see or underrated, plus the difference between where the player is and where they could be. Best when the gap grows while they wait.
   b. Sample or trial: a real piece of the product, limited by number of uses, quantity or time, so losing it creates the want.
   c. One step of many: the first step of a multi-step process; the remaining steps need the program.
4. Choose how it is delivered, or combine: a tool (quiz, score, calculator), information (short guide, mini course, template, video), a service (free work by a coach, for qualified parents only), or a physical item (only if the right parent wants it and asking for it qualifies them).
5. Qualify before giving anything that costs coach time: add one or two questions (player age, team level, goal) and send qualified parents to the magnet and the call, everyone else to something that fits them. The ideal lead has the budget, makes the decision, clearly needs it, and wants to start now.
6. For a service magnet, do the cost check: coach cost per magnet times the number given away per family who joins equals the cost to get one family. Proceed only if that is well under what a family is worth.
7. Name it for the result, never the vehicle. Write four to six names, using these formats:
   - number + outcome + time frame
   - how to [good thing] without [bad thing], even if [worry]
   - the [adjective] [thing] that [good thing]
   - X mistakes that keep [player] from [good thing]
8. Test the names before building much. With budget: small split-tested ads on the headline. Without: an Instagram story poll that says who should answer ("parents of club players only"), a post asking for comment A or B, or texting a few real parents. Test the wrapper harder than the contents; the name moves response more than anything inside, as long as the contents clearly solve the problem.
9. Write the call to action in three parts: clear, not clever; the exact next action; a reason to do it now. Place it before the magnet, inside it, and after it, not once. Give value between asks so each ask is welcome.
10. Follow up after they use it: "You saw [result]. The next step is [call]." Contact them and offer the assessment call.
11. If a magnet flops, change the magnet, starting with the name. One bad magnet does not prove the tactic fails.

## Rules

- Time first, money later: a parent who invests a few minutes now is more likely to pay later.
- Most visitors are not ready to buy. "Book a call" alone is for warm, informed parents; everyone else gets the magnet first.
- Sell at the point of greatest want: the magnet should leave them wanting the paid thing, never fully satisfied.
- When revealing a problem, be very specific about the gap and plainer about the result. This keeps promises realistic.
- A "watch on your own time, here is what we did" piece beats anything that reads as a sales pitch to a sophisticated parent.
- Scarcity: a coaching business has a real capacity limit. Say it plainly, or start new players in rolling groups ("we start N new players each week"). Any reason to act now beats none, but at Strive the reason and any number must be true (see Strive rules).

## Examples

From the video, in my own words:
- A free website speed test shows a slow site, puts a number on the lost sales, and sells the fix.
- His best-converting ad: "4 reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency" (the mistakes format).
- He swapped a webinar for a free case study video with a specific result in the headline, and his calendar filled the next morning.
- Gym class renamed from a "six-week deadlift and squat seminar" to the result the clients wanted; same content, far more signups.
- Four free months of done-for-you service, then paid once clients were used to it.
- Laser hair removal: one session sold as the first of six to eight.
- He trained people free for a year for testimonials; almost all then paid.

For Strive, MY SUGGESTIONS (not from the video), each pointing at the assessment call:
- Reveal a problem: a weak foot test the player does at home in a minute (touches with the strong foot, then the weak foot) that shows the gap, with "book a call and we'll show you the plan to close it".
- One step of many: one real session from a Strive week, sent after a short form with the qualifying questions.
- Information, mistakes format: "X mistakes that keep club players stuck at the same level" (framed as a complement to their club, never against it).
- Service: a coach looks at one clip of the player and sends back the one thing to fix first, for qualified families only.

## Strive Rules (from CLAUDE.md, apply to every draft)

- Say "your player", never "your child" or "your kid".
- No em dashes. Never "AI", "automated" or "algorithm" in anything a parent sees. Say "we" when Coach Gary is in it.
- Use only confirmed prices, dates and numbers; invent none. Any capacity or scarcity number must be one Coach Yinka confirms is real.
- No alumni names in public pieces (calls and the VSL only).
- Position Strive as a trusted complement to the player's club, never "your club failed you".
- Never label a testimonial "real" or "verified".
- Never promise a roster spot, a scholarship or an academy place.
- Draft only. Coach Yinka approves before anything is posted, sent or built into the app.

## Mistakes To Avoid

- A "book a call" or "get a quote" button as the only option for lukewarm followers.
- Giving away the core solution for free.
- Naming the vehicle (drills, sessions, an app) instead of the result.
- Building a great magnet and forgetting the call to action, or asking only once.
- Asking over and over without value between asks.
- Spending coach time on parents who were never qualified.
- Deciding lead magnets don't work after one bad one.
- Made-up urgency. The video says even a nonsense reason lifts response; Strive's rule is that public scarcity and reasons must be real, so only true reasons are used.
