Rule of the ladder: pass the Beginner, then the Advanced, then move up.
A pass mark is in a row, no drops; a drop ends the attempt and the
count starts again.

## Why this order (clinical, from the footage)

- Catch drills before continuous drills inside every level: the catch
  is the reset. A player who cannot do one clean touch and catch has
  no business keeping it up, so Level 1 is drop, touch, catch and its
  Advanced is the same touch sent above the head, which demands a
  clean contact and a judged drop before any rhythm is asked for.
- Two touches (Level 2) before continuous (Level 3): the second foot
  meeting a ball the first foot set is the whole skill of juggling,
  isolated. The Advanced puts both touches on the weaker foot.
- Keep it up at knee height (Level 3 Beginner) before Low and tight
  (Advanced): the low version gives less time per touch and is the
  classic control drill; the footage shows it at shin height, 17 s
  unbroken, visibly tighter than the knee-height take.
- Height (Level 4) after rhythm: once the ball stays up, the player
  learns to choose how hard to hit it. Waist first, then every touch
  to head height.
- Thigh (Level 5) after the feet levels: new surface, same catch-first
  rule. Drop, thigh, catch is the gentlest thigh drill; foot to thigh
  adds the timing between surfaces.
- Thigh Control (Level 6): two thigh touches with a catch, then thighs
  only continuous.
- Thighs High (7), Feet and Thighs (8), Head (9): each one filmed
  advanced only; ordered by how many things can go wrong at once
  (height on one surface, then switching surfaces mid-run, then the
  hardest surface).

## The copy (player-facing, Coach Yinka's voice)

### Keep It Up
### The Juggling Ladder

Juggling is the fastest way to a better first touch. Every level on
this ladder is one small thing done cleanly, filmed so you can see
exactly what passing looks like. Nine levels, easiest to hardest.
Every level has a pass mark. You hit the mark, you move up.

How it works

1. Start at Level 1, even if you can already juggle. The early levels
   are where the clean contact gets built.
2. Every level has a Beginner drill and an Advanced drill. Pass the
   Beginner, then the Advanced, then move up. From Level 7 the drills
   are advanced only.
3. A pass mark is in a row, with no drops. If you drop it, that attempt
   is over and the count starts again.
4. Five minutes a day is enough. Juggling pays you back for every day,
   not for one long day.
5. Film the day you pass a level. In two months you will want to see
   where you started.

Level 1, First Touch. What you are building: one clean contact with the
laces, ball coming straight back up.
- Beginner, Drop, touch, catch. Ball in your hands. Drop it, one touch
  with your strong foot, catch it at your chest. Repeat. Cue: toe up,
  ankle locked, hit the middle of the ball. Pass: 10 in a row, caught
  at your chest without reaching.
- Advanced, Drop, high touch, catch. Same drill, but the one touch goes
  above your head and you catch it on the way down. Cue: same contact,
  more leg, eyes on the ball the whole way. Pass: 10 in a row.

Level 2, Two Touches. What you are building: the second foot meeting a
ball the first foot set.
- Beginner, Drop, two touches, catch. Drop it, right foot, left foot,
  catch. Repeat. Cue: the first touch is small, the second one sets up
  the catch. Pass: 10 in a row.
- Advanced, Weak foot, two touches, catch. Both touches with your
  weaker foot, then catch. Cue: slow down, the weak foot needs a
  second longer. Pass: 10 in a row.

Level 3, Keep It Up. What you are building: rhythm with no reset.
- Beginner, Keep it up, both feet. Drop it and keep it going, both
  feet, ball around your knees, standing in one spot. Cue: small
  touches, stay on the balls of your feet. Pass: 20 touches without a
  drop.
- Advanced, Low and tight. Keep it up with the ball never above your
  knee. Cue: quick feet, quiet touches, do not let it climb. Pass: 20
  touches without a drop.

Level 4, Height. What you are building: control of how hard you hit
it.
- Beginner, Waist height. Keep it up with every touch reaching your
  waist, both feet. Cue: same contact every time, let it drop, do not
  chase it. Pass: 20 touches without a drop.
- Advanced, Head height. Keep it up with every touch reaching your
  head, standing still. Cue: lock the ankle, strike through the ball,
  wait for it. Pass: 10 touches without a drop.

Level 5, Thigh. What you are building: a second surface.
- Beginner, Drop, thigh, catch. Drop it onto your thigh, let it pop
  up, catch. Repeat. Cue: knee up, thigh flat like a table, ball lands
  on the middle. Pass: 10 in a row.
- Advanced, Drop, foot, thigh, catch. Drop it, one foot touch up to
  your thigh, thigh, catch. Cue: the foot touch is soft, it only needs
  to reach the thigh. Pass: 10 in a row.

Level 6, Thigh Control. What you are building: the thigh as a surface
you trust.
- Beginner, Drop, foot, thigh, thigh, catch. Drop it, foot, thigh,
  thigh, catch. Cue: the first thigh has to be clean or the second one
  never comes. Pass: 10 in a row.
- Advanced, Thighs only, keep it up. Keep it up on your thighs only,
  both legs, no feet. Cue: stay tall, bring the knee to the ball, do
  not lean back. Pass: 20 thigh touches without a drop.

Level 7, Thighs High. Advanced only. Keep it up on your thighs with
every touch sent to head height. Cue: drive the knee, catch it on the
flat of the thigh, wait for it to come down. Pass: 15 without a drop.

Level 8, Feet and Thighs. Advanced only. Keep it up switching between
feet and thighs without stopping. Cue: let the height tell you the
surface, low is feet, high is thighs. Pass: 30 touches without a
drop, switching surface at least five times.

Level 9, Head. Advanced only. Keep it up with your forehead. Cue: eyes
open, forehead not the top of the head, knees soft. Pass: 10 headers
without a drop.

Level 10, Everything. Coming when it is filmed: feet, thighs and head
in one run.
