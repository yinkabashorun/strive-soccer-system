# Strive Elite guarantee: every Hormozi variation, scored for our exact situation

Written Oct 14 2026 for Coach Yinka. Source: $100M Offers, the
"Enhancing the Offer: Guarantees" chapter (four types, the conditional
sub-list, stacking, the "if you don't X in Y, then Z" formula). Every
number below marked PROPOSAL is mine to propose and yours to stamp;
nothing here is live until phase C.

## What a guarantee has to do for us, specifically

Three jobs, in this order:

1. Kill the parent's real fear. From the Oct 11 problem list, the two
   biggest are "who makes him do it" and "I can't tell if it's working".
   A guarantee that answers those two converts. One that answers "what if
   I want my money back" answers a fear they don't have yet.
2. Filter, not attract. The family we want is the one that will do four
   sessions a week. A guarantee whose conditions ARE the behavior we
   want makes the right family say "easy" and the wrong family say
   "that's a lot". Hormozi's point: the condition is the product doing
   its job.
3. Be enforceable from the app, not from memory. We now track completed
   drills with timestamps, time trained, progress videos every two
   weeks, clips sent, check-ins, and breaks. Any condition we state must
   be something the app can show to the parent and to you.

Facts the guarantee has to live inside: Strive Elite is month to month,
$249 locked for life by Nov 1, $500 after (decided Oct 12). Current rule
is a same-day refund, gracious, always (ads, VSL and /demo say "same-day
refund"). The player does the work alone at home; Coach Yinka builds the
week and answers clips. There is no film room in Elite. Coach Yinka's
time is the scarce thing, so any guarantee that costs his hours has to
be capped.

## The four types, every variation, scored

Score: fit for Strive Elite out of 5, with the reason.

### 1. Unconditional

- Money-back, no questions. Hormozi: strongest headline, most abuse,
  works best on low-ticket or high-margin. Fit 2/5 as the ONLY
  guarantee: it answers no real fear, it attracts tire-kickers, and a
  parent who wants out after two weeks of the kid not training gets paid
  to quit. Fit 4/5 as a SHORT first window (see the stack below).
- Satisfaction-based ("if you're not happy"). Fit 2/5. Same problem,
  vaguer.

### 2. Conditional (do X, get Y). Hormozi's list, one by one.

- Outsized refund (give back more than they paid, e.g. double). Fit
  1/5. We have real costs (your time) and no margin story that makes
  "double your money back" believable to a parent. Reads like a gimmick
  next to a coach.
- Service guarantee (we keep working free until the result). Fit 3/5.
  Honest and strong, but open-ended months of a free $249 seat is a real
  cost and it does not filter anyone.
- Modified service (if it fails you get an upgraded version free). Fit
  4/5 as a component: "you get me one on one" IS the upgrade a parent
  values most, and it costs you nothing unless the kid did all the work
  and still did not improve, which almost never happens.
- Credit-based refund (refund as credit, not cash). Fit 3/5 for
  in-person families (credit toward 1:1s or Friday group) but weak for
  remote families who can only use the app.
- Personal service guarantee (I work with you personally until it
  works). Fit 5/5. This is the one. It is the thing you already do, it
  is capped by its own condition (only families who did every session
  qualify), and it is the strongest possible "I stand behind this" a
  coach can say. Cost: a handful of Zoom sessions in the rare case it
  triggers.
- Hotel plus airfare perks (refund plus what it cost to show up). Fit
  0/5. Nothing to travel to.
- Wage-payment (we pay you for your time). Fit 0/5. We are not going to
  pay a 13-year-old for training.
- Release of service (walk away from the contract if we don't deliver).
  Fit 1/5 today because month to month already IS a release of service.
  It becomes 4/5 the moment pay-in-full exists (item 6): "pay for the
  season, and if we miss the mark you are released from the rest." That
  is how you make pay-in-full feel safe.
- Delayed second payment (don't pay the rest until the first result).
  Fit 3/5. Month to month has no "second payment". It fits pay-in-full:
  half now, half at week 6 once the parent sees the first progress
  clips. Worth keeping for phase C.
- First outcome (pay nothing until the first milestone). Fit 4/5 as the
  opening move. Your intake item 4 is exactly this: "see before you pay,
  week 1 delivered". The version that fits Stripe month to month: you
  are charged on day one, and if week 1 is not in the app and the first
  session is not what we said, it is refunded in full, no call needed.
  That is "first outcome" in refund form.

### 3. Anti-guarantee ("all sales are final")

- Hormozi: use it when the value is the access itself and refunds would
  get abused; it has to come with a reason the buyer respects. Fit 2/5
  for the program (parents of minors need an out) but 5/5 for ONE thing:
  the price lock. "The $249 rate is yours for life as long as you stay.
  If you cancel and come back, you come back at the current price." That
  is an anti-guarantee that filters out the pause-and-return family and
  makes the lock feel real. No refund language needed.

### 4. Implied (performance-based pricing)

- Revenue share, profit share, pay-per-result, ratchets, milestone
  bonuses. Fit 1/5. There is no money result in youth development to
  share, and "pay per goal scored" is exactly the promise we refuse to
  make. The one implied form that works for us is the ratchet in
  reverse: the founding lock. You never pay more as long as you stay.

### Hormozi's structural rules we must obey

- "If you don't get X in Y time, then Z." X must be something a parent
  can see. Y must be long enough for the work to show and short enough
  to feel near. Z must be something you can honor without flinching.
- Stack them: a short unconditional window to remove the buying risk,
  then a long conditional one that rewards the work.
- The bigger the condition, the bigger the promise can be. Conditions
  should be the behaviors the product needs anyway.
- Never guarantee what you do not control. We do not control a roster
  spot, a starting spot, a tryout, or "dominant on his team" (flagged
  Oct 11). We do control sessions, filmed drills, the plan, and the
  progress clips.

## The recommendation: a three-layer stack

### Layer 1, buying risk: the Week 1 Promise (first outcome, unconditional)

PROPOSAL: "Your player's first week is in the app within one day of
joining. If it is not, or if you open it and it is not what we showed
you, you get every dollar back the same day. No call, no form."

- Replaces the bare "same-day refund". Same cost (we already do it),
  but now it says WHAT we are guaranteeing, which is Hormozi's whole
  point. It also keeps the gracious-refund rule intact in spirit.
- Window: PROPOSAL 7 days from week 1 going live, then layer 2 takes
  over. You decide the number.

### Layer 2, result risk: the Proof Guarantee (conditional, personal
### service, parent-judged)

PROPOSAL, exact language: "Do every session for 12 weeks and film the
progress clip every two weeks like the app asks. If you watch his week
2 clip next to his week 12 clip and cannot see the difference, I refund
all three months and I coach him myself on Zoom until you can."

Why this is the high converter and the filter at the same time:
- The parent judges. Not us, not a stat line. Nothing to argue about,
  and it is the exact "I can't tell if it's working" fear, answered with
  a thing they will literally watch.
- The conditions are the product. Four sessions a week in the app
  (timestamped, visible to you and to them on the home screen), the
  progress clip every second week (already asks, already texts the
  parent the comparison). A family that hears "do every session" and
  flinches is the family you do not want. A family that hears it and
  says "that's what we want him doing anyway" is a founding family.
- The consequence is your strongest card, not your weakest. Three
  months back AND you personally. A parent reads that as "he is that
  sure", which is what it is. Cost if it ever triggers: $747 and some
  Zoom hours on a kid who did 48 sessions and filmed six clips. If that
  kid did not visibly improve, you want to be on that Zoom anyway.
- Breaks do not break it. A break (already built) pauses the clock; the
  12 weeks are 12 weeks of training, not 12 calendar weeks. Say so.
- "Every session" needs a definition. PROPOSAL: every assigned session
  in the app marked done, extras not required, a missed week allowed
  only as a break set in the app. Alternative if you want it softer:
  at least 44 of 48. Your number.

What the app must do to make this airtight (not built, small):
- A guarantee tracker on the parent-facing side: weeks completed in
  full out of 12, clips filmed out of 6, "on track for the Proof
  Guarantee" or "one missed week". Same data as today's time-trained
  strip, just framed as the guarantee.
- The same tracker on your player page so a refund request is a glance,
  never a debate.

### Layer 3, the lock: the Founding Rate anti-guarantee

PROPOSAL: "$249 a month is yours for life as long as you stay. Cancel
and come back later and you come back at whatever the price is then."

- Makes the lock a real thing with a real cost to leaving, with no
  refund language and nothing to enforce beyond Stripe.
- Filters the pause-and-return family without saying no to anyone.

## What this changes, and what it does not

- The standing "same-day refund, gracious, always" rule stays as how we
  behave. Layer 1 just gives it a name and a reason. If a founding
  family asks for money back on day 20 for a reason that is not the
  guarantee, you still decide case by case, same as today.
- The ads, the VSL and /demo say "same-day refund". Layer 1 is
  compatible with all of that copy; layers 2 and 3 are new copy and go
  live with the phase C page rewrite and the $500 merge, not before.
- The VSL cannot be re-cut; the Proof Guarantee can sit as an on-screen
  card under section 6 or in the page copy beneath the video.
- Nothing in any layer promises a roster, a spot, or "dominant".

## Variations I rejected on purpose, so they stay rejected

- "Double your money back": gimmick next to a coach, and a parent's
  refund math is not why they buy.
- "Results in 30 days or free": too short for visible change in a
  13-year-old, and it pays families to quit at the exact moment the work
  starts to compound.
- "Unlimited 1:1 until he makes the team": guarantees what we do not
  control and eats your calendar.
- "No refunds": wrong for parents of minors and kills the funnel on the
  first objection.

## Decisions for you

1. Layer 1 window: 7 days from week 1 live, or something else.
2. Layer 2 term: 12 weeks (matches your "12 weeks dominant" notebook
   line in a form we can keep) or 8.
3. "Every session" strict, or 44 of 48.
4. Consequence: refund plus Zoom, or Zoom only (keeps the money, still
   strong, slightly lower conversion).
5. Layer 3 as written, or keep the lock unconditional.

as the next app item.

The 2-Week Proof Guarantee, his shape: "Film your player doing the
freestyle dribbling drill on day one. Do every session for two weeks.
Film the same drill again. If you watch the two clips back to back and
don't see him getting better, you get your money back."

- Same drill for everyone: 8-cone freestyle (the bank's freestyle
  dribbling drill). The coach can pick a different one per player.
- The guarantee is NOT in the app. No tracker, no refund language,
  nothing a player sees. It lives in the sales conversation and, in
  phase C, on the page. The app only does the clips, cleanly: a first
  clip on day one ("Your first clip"), then the same drill every second
  week ("Week N clip"), the parent texted each time with the previous
  clip for comparison.
- Still his to stamp before the copy is written: the work condition
  (8 of 8 or 7 of 8 sessions in the first two weeks), the claim window,
  and that the payout is the first month's $249.
