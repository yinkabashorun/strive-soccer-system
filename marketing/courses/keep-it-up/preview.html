The Juggling Ladder

Juggling is the fastest way to a better first touch. Every level on this ladder is one small thing done cleanly, filmed so you can see exactly what passing looks like. Nine levels, easiest to hardest. Every level has a pass mark. You hit the mark, you move up.

How it works
  1. Start at Level 1, even if you can already juggle. The early levels are where the clean contact gets built.
  2. Every level has a Beginner drill and an Advanced drill. Pass the Beginner, then the Advanced, then move up. From Level 7 the drills are advanced only.
  3. A pass mark is in a row, with no drops. If you drop it, that attempt is over and the count starts again.
  4. Five minutes a day is enough. Juggling pays you back for every day, not for one long day.
  5. Film the day you pass a level. In two months you will want to see where you started.
1

First Touch

What you are building: one clean contact with the laces, ball coming straight back up.

Beginner

Drop, touch, catch

Ball in your hands. Drop it, one touch with your strong foot, catch it at your chest. Repeat.

Cue: toe up, ankle locked, hit the middle of the ball.

Pass: 10 in a row, caught at your chest without reaching.

Advanced

Drop, high touch, catch

Same drill, but the one touch goes above your head and you catch it on the way down.

Cue: same contact, more leg, eyes on the ball the whole way.

Pass: 10 in a row.

2

Two Touches

What you are building: the second foot meeting a ball the first foot set.

Beginner

Drop, two touches, catch

Drop it, right foot, left foot, catch. Repeat.

Cue: the first touch is small, the second one sets up the catch.

Pass: 10 in a row.

Advanced

Weak foot, two touches, catch

Both touches with your weaker foot, then catch.

Cue: slow down, the weak foot needs a second longer.

Pass: 10 in a row.

3

Keep It Up

What you are building: rhythm with no reset.

Beginner

Keep it up, both feet

Drop it and keep it going, both feet, ball around your knees, standing in one spot.

Cue: small touches, stay on the balls of your feet.

Pass: 20 touches without a drop.

Advanced

Low and tight

Keep it up with the ball never above your knee.

Cue: quick feet, quiet touches, do not let it climb.

Pass: 20 touches without a drop.

4

Height

What you are building: control of how hard you hit it.

Beginner

Waist height

Keep it up with every touch reaching your waist, both feet.

Cue: same contact every time, let it drop, do not chase it.

Pass: 20 touches without a drop.

Advanced

Head height

Keep it up with every touch reaching your head, standing still.

Cue: lock the ankle, strike through the ball, wait for it.

Pass: 10 touches without a drop.

5

Thigh

What you are building: a second surface.

Beginner

Drop, thigh, catch

Drop it onto your thigh, let it pop up, catch. Repeat.

Cue: knee up, thigh flat like a table, ball lands on the middle.

Pass: 10 in a row.

Advanced

Drop, foot, thigh, catch

Drop it, one foot touch up to your thigh, thigh, catch.

Cue: the foot touch is soft, it only needs to reach the thigh.

Pass: 10 in a row.

6

Thigh Control

What you are building: the thigh as a surface you trust.

Beginner

Drop, foot, thigh, thigh, catch

Drop it, foot, thigh, thigh, catch.

Cue: the first thigh has to be clean or the second one never comes.

Pass: 10 in a row.

Advanced

Thighs only, keep it up

Keep it up on your thighs only, both legs, no feet.

Cue: stay tall, bring the knee to the ball, do not lean back.

Pass: 20 thigh touches without a drop.

7

Thighs High

Advanced only. Height on the thigh.

Advanced

Thighs high, keep it up

Keep it up on your thighs with every touch sent to head height.

Cue: drive the knee, catch it on the flat of the thigh, wait for it to come down.

Pass: 15 without a drop.

8

Feet and Thighs

Advanced only. Switching surface without stopping.

Advanced

Feet and thighs, keep it up

Keep it up switching between feet and thighs without stopping.

Cue: let the height tell you the surface, low is feet, high is thighs.

Pass: 30 touches without a drop, switching surface at least five times.

9

Head

Advanced only. The hardest surface.

Advanced

Head it up

Keep it up with your forehead.

Cue: eyes open, forehead not the top of the head, knees soft.

Pass: 10 headers without a drop.

10

Everything

Not filmed yet. Feet, thighs and head in one run. One clip of about ten seconds makes this the 10-Level Juggling Ladder.

Preview notes for Coach Yinka, not part of the course

Decisions made from the footage

  1. Every take that held a beginner and an advanced half (clips 1, 2 and 7) was split at the last catch. The single-drill clips from the second shoot were paired by skill, not by file order.
  2. Clip 1's second half and clip 2's second half are the same drill (keep it up, both feet, knee height). Clip 2's take is the demo because it starts from a drop out of the hands and runs longer. Clip 1's half is a spare take, not in the bank.
  3. Clip 9 is the right foot and clip 11 is the left foot, read from which leg lifts while he stands side-on. Clip 11 is named "Weak foot" so it reads right for a left-footed player.
  4. Clip 10 ends with one touch popped to head height; the demo is cut just before it and the drill is waist height. If that pop was the point, say so and it becomes "juggle, pop one high, settle" with the clip re-cut.
  5. Clip 4 is two drills, split where the surface changes. Its first half (head height) is the shortest demo on the ladder at 4.3 seconds.
  6. Nine levels from twelve clips. Level 10 is one more clip. The subtitle reads "The Juggling Ladder" until it exists.
  7. Pass marks are proposals. Change any number and the page and the bank rows follow.
  8. All fifteen drills are in the drill bank under Ball Mastery with the "Juggling:" prefix, filmed, few yards, no wall, switched OFF so the weekly builder does not draft them into live weeks until you turn on the ones you want at /coach/drills.
