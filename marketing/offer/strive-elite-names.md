# Strive Elite: naming (step 4 of the offer build, Hormozi's MAGIC)

Written Oct 14 2026 on Coach Yinka's "we need to name the offer better".
The aim it names is in strive-elite-aim.md (12 to 15 year old club
player chasing the next level, parent who sees the weak foot and first
touch not moving). Nothing here is live until he stamps it; the names
go on with the phase C page rewrite.

## The rule set (from the book and his podcast episodes on it)

M A G I C: Make a magnetic reason why, Announce the avatar, Give them a
goal, Indicate a time interval, Complete with a container word. Three to
five of the five, never all of them, or the name gets too long. The name
is the WRAPPER: it can change when it fatigues, the product underneath
does not. "Strive Elite" is the product and the app; the offer name
wraps it. Every bonus in the stack gets its own name too, because a
named thing has a value and an unnamed thing is a feature.

Our own rules on top: no promise we don't control (no "starter", no
"roster", no "scholarship", "dominant" is call language only), "your
player" never "your child", no AI words, no em dashes, "we" wherever
Gary is in the picture, nothing invented.

What the competition names theirs, for contrast: the at-home programs
that showed up in a search are generic libraries ("At-Home Training",
"At Home Videos", per-user app subscriptions) with no coach and no week
built for the kid. Our name should carry the one thing none of them
can say: a coach builds the week.

## The headline name, three options

1. The Next Level Program (recommended). Avatar + goal + container, with
   "12-Week" or "Founding" slotted in where the page or ad needs the
   reason why. Full form on the page: "Strive Elite: The 12-Week Next
   Level Program for Club Players." Short form everywhere else: "The
   Next Level Program". Why: "next level" is the goal four of five
   buyers wrote in their own words (better club, academy, D1, pro) and
   it promises direction, not a result we don't control. "12 weeks"
   matches his notebook ("12 weeks dominant, 6 weeks confident") and
   the two-week proof clip sits inside it cleanly (week 1 clip, week 3
   clip, week 13 clip). It also survives a price change and a season
   change, so it does not fatigue in a month.
2. The Club Player's Week. Avatar + container, no promise at all.
   Strongest at saying what the thing IS (a week, built for a club
   player, every Monday) and the safest copy on the planet. Weaker as
   a magnet: no goal, no clock. Keep it as the sub-line under option 1:
   "A week built for your club player, every Monday."
3. The Tryout-Ready 12 Weeks. Goal + interval, the most magnetic of
   the three, and the most dangerous: "tryout-ready" edges toward a
   roster promise, and we do not know every club's tryout month, so
   "12 weeks before tryouts" cannot be printed without the dates. Call
   language, or a seasonal M-word later ("the Tryout Season cohort")
   once a real date exists.

The founding window as the M-word: "Founding Families" is already a GHL
tag and the VSL says it. Use it as the reason why on the price line
("Founding rate, $249 locked for life by Nov 1"), not in the program
name, so the name outlives Nov 1.
