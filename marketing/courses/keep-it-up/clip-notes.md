# Keep It Up, The 10-Level Juggling Ladder: clip notes

Running notes on every clip Coach Yinka sends for the juggling course
(Oct 22 2026 onward). Each clip is read frame by frame here before
anything is named or built. Order, names and pass marks are PROPOSALS
until Coach Yinka approves the preview. Source files are the phone
originals (HEVC 1080x1920 portrait, bt709, 30 fps); encode recipe is
the Oct 21 standard (H.264 720x1280, crf 27, maxrate 3500k, faststart).

Pattern so far: each take holds TWO variations, beginner first then the
step up, with a visible reset between them. Split at the reset.

## Clip 1: IMG_7292.mov (14.9 s), Coach Yinka: "That's #1"

- 0.0 to ~8.0 s, BEGINNER: ball in hands, drop, ONE touch with the right
  foot, catch. Repeat. Standing still, ball never above the waist.
  Difficulty: the very first rung. One touch, one catch, no rhythm
  needed yet.
- ~8.0 to 14.9 s, STEP UP: continuous juggling, both feet, ball kept low
  (below the knee), no catching, standing in one spot.
  Difficulty: first continuous run. Low ball = short reaction time but
  small movements; both feet already.
- Working names: "Drop, touch, catch" / "Keep it up low, both feet".
- Split point: 8.0 s (ball goes from hands to continuous). Trim: start
  0.2 s, end at 14.7 s.

## Clip 2: IMG_7293.mov (14.2 s)

- 0.0 to ~6.0 s, BEGINNER: ball in hands, drop, TWO touches (right then
  left, the second one sent higher), catch. Repeat. He drifts a step or
  two between reps; the second touch is bigger than clip 1's.
  Difficulty: rung above clip 1's first half. Two touches before the
  catch means the second foot has to meet a ball the first foot set.
- ~6.5 to 14.2 s, STEP UP: continuous juggling, alternating feet, ball
  around knee height, hands at the sides, steady rhythm, no catching.
  Reads the same family as clip 1's second half; if anything the ball
  sits a touch higher and the rhythm is steadier. FLAG for Coach Yinka:
  is this meant as the same drill as clip 1's second half (then one of
  them is the demo and the other is spare) or a distinct rung (e.g.
  "alternate every touch" vs "any foot")? Could not tell from footage
  alone.
- Working names: "Drop, two touches, catch" / "Alternate feet, knee
  height".
- Split point: ~6.3 s (last catch, then he drops it and keeps going).
  Trim: start 0.2 s, end 14.0 s.
