## The eight findings that change the plan

1. **Sign-in is written by script, which is the weak way on iPhone.** `LoginForm.tsx` signs in with the browser Supabase client, so the session cookie is written with `document.cookie`. WebKit caps script-written cookies at 7 days; server-set cookies are not capped. A member who skips a week can be signed out. Fix before the first build (B2).
2. **Same-site links with `target="_blank"` leave the app.** Capacitor hands every new-window request to Safari, including `/terms` and `/privacy` on the agreement screen. A reviewer's first minute would bounce to Safari (B5).
3. **The new guideline 2.5.19 (Oct 16 2026) bites a live-site shell.** Features added to the site after approval reach the app unreviewed. New member-facing features need to stay hidden inside the app until a build whose review notes describe them is approved (B1).
4. **"Specific business" rejection (guideline 3.2) is a second real risk, beside 3.1.1.** Apple rejects apps "intended to be used by a specific business or organization, including partners, clients, or employees" that chose public distribution. Unlisted App Distribution is Apple's own answer and fits an invite-only product (B10).
5. **A built-in example mode does not replace a demo account.** Guideline 2.1(a) allows it only "with prior approval by Apple" and only when a demo account cannot be given (B10).
6. **Push banners fire while the app is open.** `presentationOptions` includes `banner` and `sound`; Apple's guidance is to stay quiet in the foreground (B11).
7. **A checkbox is not verifiable parental consent, but two things already in the sales process are listed methods**: a card payment tied to the consent, and a video call with trained staff. They need to be connected to the consent record (B8).
8. **Apple says the age laws changed nothing in App Review.** What review does enforce is the age-rating questionnaire, and "Messaging and Chat" must be answered Yes (B8).

Corrections to `docs/app-store/README.md`: Texas is right (Apple applied SB 2420 to new Texas accounts from June 4 2026). "Utah and Louisiana are delayed to 2027" is partly supported: Louisiana is reported reset to July 1 2027; sources conflict on Utah (see B8). The line "Add [a privacy manifest] in Xcode if the upload warns" can be firmer (B7). The wake lock line can be closed (B3).

## B1. A shell that loads the live site through `server.url`

**Answer.** No Apple rule bans an app whose screens come from the developer's own server; the test is guideline 4.2: "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website." Apple's App Review page adds: "Websites served in an iOS app, web content that is not formatted for iOS, and limited web interactions do not make a quality app." Capacitor's own config documentation says of `server.url`: "This is intended for use with live-reload servers. **This is not intended for use in production.**" (also said of `allowNavigation`). Developers report 4.2 is judged on what the reviewer experiences, not on the plugin list: a Capacitor app with native location, share sheet and custom Swift plugins was still rejected because its core flow ran in a third-party web page. Two rules matter more for a live-site shell than 4.2 itself: 2.5.2 (an app may not "download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app") and the new 2.5.19, which Apple describes as prohibiting "adding features or functionality that weren't included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review."

**What approved ones have in common** (REPORTED, vendor and developer accounts, not Apple): a native feature reachable from the first screen (push, camera, haptics); no browser chrome, cookie banners, web footers or "open in browser" dead ends; a real offline or error screen; nothing that drops into Safari in normal use; review notes in plain words that name what the app does that the website cannot; working demo logins.

**Is bundling materially safer?** Yes, on three counts: the app paints instantly and offline; the "not for production" setting is gone; and what was reviewed is what ships (2.5.2, 2.5.19). It is not a guarantee, because 4.2 is about the experience. For this app (server-rendered Next.js with server actions, cookie auth and middleware) bundling means a rewrite to a static client plus an API, so it is not a first-draft option.

**What to do here.**
- Stay on `server.url` for version 1. Keep the bundled offline page.
- Adopt a rule and a guard: a new member-facing feature stays hidden when `inNativeApp()` is true until an App Store build whose review notes describe it is approved. Bug fixes and content (new weeks, drills, course pages) are fine.
- Say plainly in the review notes that the app's screens are served from the developer's own site, and list every difference between app and website (no account creation in the app, the course card behaviour). A developer who removed a feature on iOS only, without saying so, got three rejections for "features that appear to have been intentionally hidden" (thread 848576).
- Fix the Safari bounces in B5 before submission.
- Small thing found in the Capacitor source: "is this our own site" is a prefix test on the string `https://thestriveapp.com` (`WebViewDelegationHandler.swift`), so a host that merely begins with those characters would load inside the web view. Writing the URL as `https://thestriveapp.com/` is worth testing on the first build.
- If rejected under 4.2: reply with the native list in plain words, then consider bundling the sign-in screen and home shell only.

**How sure.** Guideline and Capacitor wording: CONFIRMED. How reviewers apply 4.2 to remote shells and what approved apps share: REPORTED. No Apple staff statement on `server.url` was found (the one forum thread asking it, June 2026, has no answer).

## B2. Staying signed in

**Answer.** The web view's default data store "saves website data persistently to disk" (Apple), and Capacitor uses that default, so a cookie with an expiry survives closing the app and restarting the phone. Three things can still sign a member out. (1) WebKit's tracking prevention caps cookies written by script (`document.cookie`) at 7 days; cookies set by the server in a response are not capped. It is reported on by default in every `WKWebView` app since iOS 14, and Capacitor developers report a forced re-login every 7 days. (2) Cookies with no expiry are dropped when iOS cleans up a suspended app. (3) Developers, including two on iOS 18.5 to 26.0, report non-expired first-party cookies vanishing from `WKWebView` after 1 to 4 weeks; Apple's engineer asked for bug reports and confirmed no cause. One report describes this exact stack (Next.js, Supabase `createBrowserClient`, Capacitor iOS, remote URL) signing out on every kill, unresolved.

**What to do here.**
- **Move sign-in to the server.** Today `LoginForm.tsx` calls `signInWithPassword` on the browser client, so the cookie is script-written (not HttpOnly, 7-day cap). Do it in a server action or route handler with the server client so the cookie arrives by `Set-Cookie` with its 400-day max-age. Same for the reset-password form.
- **Widen the middleware matcher.** `middleware.ts` covers `/dashboard`, `/homework`, `/progress`, `/coach`, `/account`, `/login`, `/signup`. It does not cover `/film`, `/courses`, `/messages`, `/onboarding` or `/`. A token refreshed while rendering one of those pages cannot be written back, so the refresh is lost. Adding them makes every refresh a server-set cookie. (My reading of the code, not a research source.)
- Do not enable `CapacitorCookies`: it exists to patch `document.cookie` for bundled apps, and one developer traced a stuck-session bug to its cookie syncing. Not needed with a real https origin.
- `WKAppBoundDomains` with `limitsNavigationsToAppBoundDomains` is not a cookie fix. Capacitor's doc warns it "blocks navigation outside the domains" in the list. Leave it off.
- Test on the first build (C4): sign in, kill the app, restart the phone, set the clock forward 8 and 30 days, reopen. If cookies still vanish, the fallback is a native copy of the session in the Keychain that the site restores at launch. Do not build it unless the test fails; Supabase's rotating refresh tokens make it fiddly.

**How sure.** Persistent store and Capacitor's use of it: CONFIRMED (Apple docs, Capacitor source). 7-day cap on script cookies and its default in `WKWebView`: REPORTED (WebKit's own posts seen only as search summaries; webkit.org unreachable). Random cookie loss: REPORTED, unexplained. That this app's cookie is script-written: CONFIRMED in the code.

## B3. Keeping the screen awake

**Answer.** Yes, `navigator.wakeLock` works inside `WKWebView`. WebKit's feature file turns "Screen Wake Lock API" on by default for `WKWebView` in every shipped branch from Safari 16.4 through the current one (I checked branches 7615 to 7623 and main). MDN's compatibility data lists iOS web views as matching Safari from 16.4. The well-known bug (fixed in iOS 18.4) was only for Home Screen web apps. In WebKit's code the lock disables the idle timer in the host app's own process, which is the case of a native shell. WebKit grants the request only after a recent tap, or if the same page was granted before, and drops the lock whenever the page is hidden.

**What to do here.**
- `keepAwake()` in `lib/elite/native.ts` already fits: `SessionPlayer` opens in the same page straight after the Start tap, and the lock is re-taken on `visibilitychange`.
- Below iOS 16.4 (the shell allows iOS 15) there is no wake lock at all. Either raise the minimum to iOS 16.4 or add the plugin.
- Belt and braces: `@capacitor-community/keep-awake` 8.0.1 (published April 2026, Capacitor 8). I read its iOS source: it sets `UIApplication.shared.isIdleTimerDisabled`. `callNative("KeepAwake", "keepAwake")` on session open and `"allowSleep"` on close would cover old iOS and any refusal. An alternative is `@capawesome/capacitor-keep-awake` (October 2026).
- Prove on a phone (C4): start a session, leave it 3 minutes with Auto-Lock at 30 seconds.

**How sure.** Enabled by default and how it is granted: CONFIRMED from WebKit source. That the screen actually stays on in this shell on each iOS version: not device-tested; one developer reports it working in iOS 16.4+ web views.

## B4. `<input type="file" accept="video/*" capture>` in the web view

**Answer.** It works, and WebKit's own code decides what opens. With `capture`, WebKit opens the camera directly (rear camera for `environment`) and offers no library. Without `capture`, it shows the menu: Photo Library, Take Photo or Video, Choose Files. The library is the system photo picker, which runs outside the app and needs no photo-library permission. The camera path needs `NSCameraUsageDescription` and, for video, `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`; both are in `Info.plist`. Library videos are re-exported before the page receives them (HEVC becomes H.264, with a "Preparing" wait and a second copy on disk). Camera recordings use the system camera sheet at its defaults, which Apple documents as medium quality and a 10-minute limit; WebKit's file does not raise them.

**What to do here.**
- Send a clip and the progress video (`capture="environment"`): camera only, small files, lower resolution than the Camera app. Good for a 30-second drill. Check the resolution on a phone; if it is too soft for coaching, drop `capture` so the member can film in the Camera app and pick from the library.
- Film Room (`multiple`, no `capture`): game clips can be large. Each one is transcoded before upload, so five long clips means a real wait with the app open. Then `uploadClip` sends one plain request with no resume; if the member switches apps mid-upload, iOS suspends the app and it likely fails. Recommended: show per-clip progress, keep the screen awake during upload, and move files over a few MB to a resumable upload (Supabase supports the TUS protocol; from memory, unverified, supabase.com unreachable).
- "Choose Files" skips the transcode, so an HEVC `.mov` can arrive untouched and will not play in Chrome on Windows or Android. Coaches on iPhone or Mac are fine.
- If a member denies the camera once, iOS does not ask again; the upload card needs one line telling them where to turn it on (Settings, Strive Elite, Camera). From memory, unverified.
- `NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription` is not needed by the picker. Keeping it is harmless.
- Test on the first build with a 100 MB+ 4K clip and with a 5-clip selection.

**How sure.** Picker behaviour, transcoding and absence of a quality override: CONFIRMED from WebKit source (main branch). Default medium quality and 10 minutes: CONFIRMED (Apple docs). Actual recorded resolution, large-file behaviour, memory: not device-tested. No photo permission for the picker: from memory, unverified.

## B5. Links that leave the app, with `allowNavigation: []`

**Answer** (read from Capacitor 8.6.2's `WebViewDelegationHandler.swift`):
- **A tapped https link to another domain**: Capacitor cancels it and calls `UIApplication.shared.open`. iOS then opens the owning app if the link is a universal link for an installed app, otherwise the default browser. The member leaves Strive Elite.
- **Any `target="_blank"` link or `window.open`, including links to thestriveapp.com itself**: Capacitor's new-window handler opens the URL with `UIApplication.shared.open` and returns no web view. So same-site links marked `_blank` open in Safari. Apple's universal-link doc adds that an app opening a universal link to its own site this way will not get it back.
- **`mailto:`**: no host, so it goes to `UIApplication.shared.open` and opens the Mail app (or nothing if no mail app is set up).
- **Zoom**: Apple documents that universal links open the owning app when another app calls `open`. Zoom join links (`zoom.us/j/...`) are reported to be registered by Zoom for its app, so Join on Zoom should open Zoom if installed, and Safari (Zoom's web page) if not. Zoom's file could not be read from here.
- Embedded frames and video sources are not affected.

**What to do here.**
- `AgreementScreen.tsx` (and `SignupForm.tsx`) link `/terms` and `/privacy` with `target="_blank"`: inside the app these jump to Safari. Drop `_blank` when `inNativeApp()` is true, or show the text in place.
- "Your clip from two weeks ago" (`ProgressVideoCard`), film links (`FilmTimeline`, `CoachFilmPanel`) and course downloads (`CourseLibrary`) are signed storage links on another domain: they open Safari. Play them in an in-app video element instead.
- Zoom buttons (`FilmRoomCard`, `CheckinBooking`, `CoachingCallsCard`) are right as they are. Test with and without Zoom installed.
- The Support page's mail link works; give the address as text too, for phones with no mail app.
- Never let `/demo` load inside the shell: it carries the Meta Pixel, and tracking inside the app would contradict the "Tracking: No" privacy label. Nothing links to it from the app today; keep it that way, or switch the pixel off for the app's user agent.

**How sure.** Capacitor's behaviour: CONFIRMED from source. Universal-link hand-off: CONFIRMED (Apple). That Zoom's `/j/` paths open the Zoom app: REPORTED; test on a phone.

## B6. Password AutoFill on the web sign-in form

**Answer.** Apple documents AutoFill for web views: the app declares an associated domain, the site serves an `apple-app-site-association` file naming the app, and the fields carry `autocomplete` values. "Password AutoFill recommends credentials only for your app's associated domain." With a remote `server.url` the page's host already is the real domain, which is the condition Capacitor's guide otherwise has to arrange with `server.hostname`. Filling saved passwords works in `WKWebView` once the association is in place; the "Save Password?" prompt does not appear for web forms in `WKWebView` (many developer reports, and Capacitor's own guide says to use a plugin for saving).

**What to do here.**
- Entitlement: add `webcredentials:thestriveapp.com` (and `applinks:thestriveapp.com` for universal links, C3) to `App.entitlements`. Needs the Team ID.
- Site: serve `/.well-known/apple-app-site-association` as JSON with no redirect, containing `"webcredentials": {"apps": ["TEAMID.com.strivesoccer.elite"]}` alongside the `applinks` block. One file serves both jobs.
- Form: `LoginForm.tsx` uses `autoComplete="email"` on the email field. Apple's page says to use `autocomplete="username"` with `type="email"` when the email is the user name. The password field (`current-password`) and the reset form (`new-password`) are right.
- Members who saved their password in Safari on thestriveapp.com should be offered it in the app after the association. That is the main win, since every account was created on the web.
- Saving from inside the app (after a password reset) needs a plugin call: `@capgo/capacitor-autofill-save-password` 8.2.6 (November 2026, Capacitor 8) is the maintained one. Optional for version 1.
- iOS fetches the association file at install through Apple's servers; test with a TestFlight build, not a debug build on a local address.

**How sure.** Requirements: CONFIRMED (Apple docs). Fill works and save prompt does not, in `WKWebView`: REPORTED. Works with remote `server.url`: follows from the docs; prove on a phone.

## B7. Privacy manifest

**Answer.** Two separate requirements exist. (1) Apple lists Capacitor and Cordova among the SDKs whose privacy manifest must be included; both ship one, and I read them in the installed 8.6.2 package: no tracking, no collected data, no required-reason APIs. (2) Any use of a required-reason API (user defaults, file timestamps, boot time, disk space, active keyboard) by the app or an SDK must be declared, or App Store Connect refuses the upload (since May 1 2024). I searched the iOS source of Capacitor core 8.6.2, push-notifications 8.1.3, haptics 8.0.2 and app 8.1.2, and `AppDelegate.swift`: none of them calls any of those APIs. So this shell should upload without an app-level manifest. Apple still expects an app to record what it collects in its own manifest, and Xcode builds its privacy report from it.

**What to do here.** Add a minimal `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` to the App target now, so it is there the day a plugin that needs a reason is added (for example `@capacitor/preferences` needs `NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults` with reason `CA92.1`):

```xml
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<!DOCTYPE plist PUBLIC "-//Apple//DTD PLIST 1.0//EN" "http://www.apple.com/DTDs/PropertyList-1.0.dtd">
<plist version="1.0">
<dict>
  <key>NSPrivacyTracking</key><false/>
  <key>NSPrivacyTrackingDomains</key><array/>
  <key>NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes</key><array/>
  <key>NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes</key><array/>
</dict>
</plist>
```

Then fill `NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes` to mirror the App Store label in README section 5 (each linked to the user, not used for tracking, purpose app functionality), choosing the values from Xcode's own list. I am not giving the identifier strings from memory, because Apple says "App Store Connect rejects app submissions that include invalid privacy manifest files." The manifest does not replace the label in App Store Connect; both are needed. If the keep-awake or save-password plugin is added, re-run the same search on its source.

**How sure.** SDK list, upload rule, Capacitor's manifests, no required-reason API use in these four packages: CONFIRMED. That an app-level file is optional for upload: follows from the docs; the first upload will show.

## B8. Age laws, the Declared Age Range API, and COPPA consent

**Answer, App Review.** Apple's age-assurance Q&A asks "Changes to App Review" and answers: "No, there are no changes to the App Review process." The same page says "developers are responsible for their own age restrictions" and, where a law applies, "you must check the age of the people using your app." So adopting the Declared Age Range API is a legal duty in some states, not a review gate. I found no report of a rejection for not adopting it. What review does enforce is the age-rating questionnaire (guideline 2.3.6): a martial-arts school app was rejected this month for answering No to "Messaging and Chat" when parents could message the school. New questionnaire items became required in September 2026 and more descriptors arrived Nov 16 2026.

**Answer, the state laws.**
- Texas SB 2420: enjoined Dec 23 2025, the injunction stayed by the Fifth Circuit on May 28 2026, the Supreme Court declined to block it on July 6 2026, merits pending. Apple: "new Apple Accounts in Texas are now subject to the law" from June 4 2026, with parental consent for downloads, purchases and "significant changes". Developer duties as summarised by law firms: rate the app, use the age category and consent signals the store provides, tell the store about significant changes; good-faith reliance on the store's information is a defence.
- Utah: Apple shares age categories for new Utah accounts from May 6 2026. Sources conflict on enforcement (one says state enforcement was removed leaving a private right of action, another says delayed to 2027).
- Louisiana: Apple announced July 1 2026; a later law (HB 977, signed May 15 2026) is reported to have moved it to July 1 2027.
- Alabama: Jan 1 2027. California AB 1043 (an operating-system age signal): Jan 1 2027.

**Minimum compliant step for this app.**
1. Answer the questionnaire truthfully: Messaging and Chat yes, user-generated content yes (private clips), no unrestricted web access. Never the Kids Category.
2. Build with the iOS 26.2 SDK or later; Apple says the age frameworks need it.
3. Before selling to families in Texas or Utah: one call to the Declared Age Range API from the shell after sign-in, passed to the site and stored, with a sensible path when the family declines to share. This is a small native plugin and needs the Apple account. Families today are in Northern Virginia, so exposure is low now; the product is sold remotely nationwide, so it will not stay zero.
4. For a significant change (new kind of data, new feature class, new payment), use PermissionKit's significant-update request, and subscribe to the consent-withdrawn server notification. Apple blocks the app from launching after a parent withdraws consent in any case.
5. A lawyer decides what counts as a significant change and whether item 3 can wait. The App Store has no way to exclude one US state.

**Answer, COPPA.** The amended rule took effect June 23 2025 with compliance by April 22 2026. Its list of accepted ways to get verifiable parental consent, 16 CFR 312.5(b)(2): a signed form returned by mail, fax or scan; a card or other payment "in connection with a monetary transaction" that notifies the account holder of each transaction; a toll-free call with trained staff; a video conference with trained staff; a government ID check; knowledge-based questions (new); a face match against photo ID (new); and, only when the child's data is not disclosed, email plus a confirming step or the new "text plus". A ticked box is not on the list, and the FTC has long said a card number without a charge is not enough.

**So for this project:** the recorded checkbox alone is not an accepted method for the member who is 12. The parent's card payment can be one, if it is tied to the consent: the parent is shown the notice (what is collected from the player, how it is used, who receives it) at or before payment, the payment is the act of consenting, and the payment reference is stored on the consent row. The sales call is also a listed method (video conference with trained staff) if the coach reads a short consent script and logs it. Doing both, and recording both in `elite_consents`, gives a defensible record. Paying outside the app is irrelevant to COPPA. The amended rule also requires a written retention policy for children's data in the notice and a written security program, and separate consent for disclosures that are not part of running the service (secondary sources). All of this is a lawyer's call, as the README says.

**How sure.** Apple's statements: CONFIRMED. Court status and other states: REPORTED, with conflicts on Utah. COPPA method list: from memory plus search summaries of the rule (ftc.gov and ecfr.gov unreachable); read 312.5(b)(2) on ecfr.gov before relying on a word of it.

## B9. Guidelines 3.1.1, 3.1.3(d), 3.1.3(e) for sign-in-only coaching apps

**Answer.** The text today: 3.1.1 bars unlocking content by "license keys" or other own mechanisms. 3.1.3(d) exempts "real-time person-to-person services between two individuals (for example tutoring students, medical consultations, real estate tours, or fitness training)" and adds "One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase." 3.1.3(e) covers services "consumed outside of the app." 3.1.3(f), the free companion app, is limited to VoIP, cloud storage, email and web hosting. 3.1.3(b), multiplatform, only helps if the content is also sold by in-app purchase.

Recent developer reports, all from Apple's forums:
- Dec 2025: a sign-in-only companion app for fitness coaches, with no prices, sign-up or links, rejected repeatedly with "Your app accesses digital content purchased outside the app, and that content is not available through in-app purchase." Unanswered.
- May 2026: a sign-in-only SaaS with every purchase word stripped out, rejected several times because reviewers saw the demo account "access subscriptions or premium content." Unanswered.
- Feb 2026: partner access codes rejected under 3.1.1; App Review replied "We believe we have resolved this issue."
- Jan 2024: a fitness app told to add in-app purchase; an Apple engineer (not App Review) read the rule as: content consumed solely in the app means in-app purchase.
- No approval or rejection of a 1:1 coaching app that argued 3.1.3(d) turned up.

So reviewers object to what the demo account can open (courses, videos, plans), not to purchase screens. The README's reading stands: the 1:1 parts fit 3.1.3(d); the course library and the group Film Room are what a reviewer can point at.

**The US rules after Epic.** The guidelines now say entitlements "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps", and 3.1.3 exempts the US storefront from the ban on encouraging other purchase methods. The Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt finding (Dec 11 2025) but said Apple may charge a cost-based commission, to be set on remand; the Supreme Court took the case (No. 25-1311) with argument expected January 2027; Apple is reported to charge nothing on link-outs meanwhile. This permits links on the US storefront. It does not clearly remove 3.1.1's demand for in-app purchase when content is unlocked inside the app. NOT CONFIRMED either way.

**Review-notes wording.** No Apple-endorsed wording exists. Developers who got through advise: plain words, concrete, who pays for what and where, cite the guideline, and book Apple's 30-minute "Meet with App Review" appointment before submitting. A draft built from the guideline's own words (mine, unproven):

A family joins after a call with a coach and pays for the coaching itself, outside the app (guideline 3.1.3(d)). Inside the app there is no account creation and the Dribbling Transformation course opens its in-app pages; on our website those differ. Player login: ... Coach login: ...

**What to do here.**
- Book the "Meet with App Review" appointment once the account is active and ask this question.
- Disclose every app-versus-web difference (see B1).

## B10. An app the public cannot use without an account

**Answer.** Four rules apply.
- **2.1(a)**: "include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login. If you are unable to provide a demo account due to legal or security obligations, you may include a built-in demo mode in lieu of a demo account with prior approval by Apple." So the example tour does not satisfy this alone. Give a real player login and a real coach login, each with a live week.
- **5.1.1(v)**: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." This app is account-based, so a sign-in wall is allowed.
- **3.2, the "specific business" rejection.** Reviewers have written (May 2025, Aug 2024): "your app is intended to be used by a specific business or organization, including partners, clients, or employees, but you've selected public distribution on the App Store... we recommend reviewing the other distribution options." An invite-only app for one coaching business's clients is close to that. Note 3.2.2(v) only bars "arbitrarily restricting who may use the app, such as by location or carrier"; paid membership is not arbitrary.
- **3.1.3**: outside the US storefront an app cannot "encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase."

**Does the example mode help?** Yes, against 4.2 and 3.2: it gives a stranger something real to do. It does not replace the demo accounts.

**Is a "request a call" link allowed?** Nothing forbids a contact form with no price. The risk is a reviewer reading "apply to join" a paid program as a call to buy elsewhere. On the US storefront, calls to action are permitted outright, so US-only distribution makes it safe by the letter. The link must not load `/demo` inside the shell (Meta Pixel, see B5): open it in Safari or use a pixel-free form.

**Options for the decision (D4).**
1. **Public listing, US only**: sign-in, "See an example week", and "Request a call" opening the web form in Safari. Findable in search. Carries the 3.2 risk; the review notes should say anyone can ask to join and is accepted after a call.
2. **Unlisted App Distribution** (Apple: for "limited audiences", "discoverable only with a direct link"): still reviewed, requested by a form, with a note in Review Notes. Fits an invite-only product, since each family gets the link from the coach, and it is Apple's own answer to the 3.2 rejection. Cost: no App Store search presence. Apple notes that anyone with the link can install, so the sign-in wall stays.
3. Start with 1 and switch to 2 if the 3.2 rejection arrives.

**How sure.** Guideline text and the unlisted programme: CONFIRMED. The 3.2 rejection wording: REPORTED (first-hand posts; Apple staff replied only with process). Whether a no-price contact link passes outside the US: NOT CONFIRMED.

## B11. Ten behaviours that make an app feel calm and rewarding

Each is checkable on a screen or in a payload. Quotes are from Apple's Human Interface Guidelines unless marked.

1. **No system permission alert before the member has done anything.** "Avoid requesting permission at launch unless the data or resource is required for your app to function." Check: fresh install, sign in, reach Home with no alert. The notification ask appears on the first finish screen (punch-list A8); the camera ask on the first Send a clip tap. HIG, Privacy: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy
2. **A screen shown before the camera or microphone alert has one button, titled Continue or Next, and no way out.** "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert"; "Don't include an option to cancel." Check: the Send a clip flow has no custom Allow or Not now screen in front of the camera alert. (Apple's list for this rule names camera, microphone, location, contacts, calendar and tracking; notifications are not on it, so a Not now on the notification card is allowed.) Same page.
3. **One notification per event, never repeated.** "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing, even if someone hasn't responded." Check: publish a week twice; one push arrives. The Nov 12 ledger already holds this for weeks; Film Room edits and coach messages need the same check. HIG, Notifications: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications
4. **Pushes inform; they do not give orders or sell.** "Avoid sending a notification that tells people to perform specific tasks within your app." "Don't use notifications to send marketing or promotional content unless people explicitly agree." Check each push text: "Week 9 is live" passes, "Go do your session" does not; no course or offer announcement by push. Guideline 4.5.4 says the same and requires the in-app off switch (built). HIG, Managing notifications: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications
5. **Honest urgency.** Time Sensitive is only for "an event that's happening now or will happen within an hour." Check the APNs payload: `lib/elite/apns.ts` sends none, so everything is the default Active, which is right; at most a "Film Room starts in an hour" reminder could ever use it. Same page.
6. **Quiet while the app is open.** "Your app's notifications don't appear when your app is in the front... present the information in a way that's discoverable but not distracting or invasive, such as incrementing a badge or subtly inserting new data into the current view." Check: with the app open, a coach message shows a dot on the Coach tab, with no banner or sound. Today `capacitor.config.ts` sets `presentationOptions: ["badge","sound","banner","list"]`, which shows a banner and plays a sound in the foreground; `["badge","list"]` matches the guidance. HIG, Notifications.
7. **The badge is a count of unread notifications and clears when read.** "Use a badge only to show people how many unread notifications they have." "Keep badges up to date." Check: the app icon shows no stale number after the list is opened; the in-app bell clears (A13). The payload sends no badge today, so the icon never shows one; that is acceptable, and never worse than a stale one. HIG, Notifications.
8. **The launch screen is the first screen with nothing on it.** "Design a launch screen that's nearly identical to the first screen"; "Avoid including text"; "Don't advertise... don't include logos or other branding elements unless they're a fixed part of your app's first screen." And "Restore the previous state when your app restarts." Check: launch shows plain black matching the app, with no logo flash; returning after a kill mid-session lands back in the session. This argues against a logo launch image (A16). HIG, Launching: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching
9. **Haptics mean one thing each, are rare, and can be switched off.** "Use system-provided haptic patterns according to their documented meanings"; "Avoid overusing haptics"; "Make haptics optional." Success is documented as "a task or action has completed." Check: a drill tick is a light impact, the finish screen is the success pattern, navigation taps have none, and Account, Vibration off silences all. HIG, Playing haptics: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics
10. **A session looks active, reads at arm's length, and ends with a summary.** "Use a distinct visual appearance to indicate an active workout"; "Make sure text is legible for when people are in motion... use large font sizes, high-contrast colors"; "Provide a summary at the end of a session." Check: drill name and sets readable from two metres with the phone on the ground; the finish screen states what was just done. And on every screen, "Show something as soon as possible" (skeletons, video posters, A6). HIG, Workouts: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/workouts and Loading: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading

Two more from the same pages that touch open items: ask for a rating only "after people have demonstrated engagement" and never during a task (the system caps the prompt at three a year), https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ratings-and-reviews ; and if the app requires an account, "write a brief, friendly description of the reasons for the requirement and its benefits. Display this message in your sign-in view", https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts (useful for the signed-out screen in B10).

**How the named apps behave.** Only two behaviours could be sourced this session, both secondary. Duolingo stops its practice reminders after about a week of being ignored, with a last message saying the reminders do not seem to be working (press and user accounts: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240618-duolingo-app-streaks ). Apple's own Activity app keeps "Goal Completions" as a separate notification switch from sharing alerts (Apple support threads: https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253212809 ). The first is worth copying: stop nudging a member who has ignored several nudges. I could not verify specific behaviours of Nike Training Club, Apple Fitness+, Strava or Headspace from a source, so none are claimed here.
